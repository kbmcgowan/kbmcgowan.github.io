Course Description

This course is designed to give students a broad introduction to the field of linguistics from the perspective of language users. As native users of at least one spoken or signed language, students are already in some ways experts in how language works. This course will explore the beliefs about and perceptions of language that they already possess and build upon this knowledge by comparing popular notions about language with those developed in linguistic science, paying attention to the contributions of popular belief as well as the differences between them and scientific findings. [This course counts for the UK Core Inquiry in the Social Sciences requirement.]

Student Learning Outcomes

After successful engagement with the material in this course, you will be able to:

  • Define and distinguish different theoretical approaches to linguistics,
  • Apply their understanding of methods and ethics of inquiry in linguistics to create and advance linguistic knowledge,
  • Demonstrate the ability to identify and use appropriate information resources in linguistics to substantiate evidence-based claims,
  • Recognize how linguistic knowledge influences society (and vice versa),
  • Identify well-formulated questions pertinent to linguistics and employ appropriate approaches to identify reasonable research strategies that could speak to that question.

Course Activities, Assignments, Exams

Course Activities and Exams

The following assignments will be used to determine your grade in the course. Below the descriptions, you will see the weight that each assignment type carries for your grade. Due dates for these assignments (subject to change) are listed in Canvas.

  • Quizzes (30%): Each week in discussion sections (beginning during the second week of classes), students will complete a short quiz in Canvas over the material that has been covered in lecture and in the course readings. These quizzes are not designed to be difficult; rather, their purpose is to ensure that everyone is keeping up with the readings and understands the material covered in the course. There will be 12 quizzes given, and the lowest two (2) quiz grades will be dropped. Each quiz is worth 3% of your final grade, for a total of 30% of the final grade. YOU WILL NEED YOUR DEVICE TO ACCESS CANVAS!
  • Observation notes (15%): Beginning in the second week of classes, students will keep an online language observation journal. More details will be provided, but students must listen to language as it is being used in their communities and document what they hear. It should not be necessary to record such interactions (and doing so surreptitiously would not be ok). Students will need to note the who, what, where, when, why, and how of what they heard, paying particular attention to what might make the interaction of interest to linguists. Observation is about careful and thoughtful attention to detail. Students must go beyond how they typically listen to conversations and try to listen like a linguist. There will be 6 entries to submit during the semester, and the lowest grade will be dropped. Each journal entry is worth 3% of your final grade, for a total of 15% of the final grade. YOU MUST NOT USE AI TO GENERATE THESE!
  • Data Collection (20%): Near the middle of the semester, students will complete a large-scale assignment involving the collection of linguistic data. More details will be provided, but students will collect data and be asked a number of questions to show that they understand aspects of linguistic research methods and ethics as explored in the class. Students will have time to work on the assignment in discussion on the due date, but the assignment will require additional time outside of class to complete. This assignment is worth 20% of the final grade.
  • Data Analysis (25%): Near the end of the semester, students will complete another large-scale assignment. This assignment will involve the analysis of the linguistic data collected near midterm. More details will be provided, but students will demonstrate their ability to identify an appropriate research question related to that data. Students will also provide relevant academic sources that could be used to better understand the data and the research question. Students will have time to work on the assignment during the scheduled exam time for their section, but the assignment will require additional time outside of the exam time to complete. This assignment is worth 25% of the final grade.
  • Participation (10%): Students are expected to attend each class (lecture and discussion) and to have done any reading or other work assigned for that day. In discussion, participation will be gauged by attendance, participation in class activities, and/or submission of assignments, as required. In lecture, participation in the class will be gauged by responses to participation questions that are sprinkled throughout the lectures. Students will be able to submit answers in class through Echo360 using a laptop, tablet, or mobile phone. The grade will be based on whether you were able to participate on a given day. If you have questions or concerns about this aspect of the course, please see the instructor immediately. Participation is worth 10% of the final grade.

Use of Generative AI Tools (e.g. LLMs such as ChatGPT, Claude, or Grammarly)

For most assignments in this course, the use of GenAI to complete any part of the submitted work is prohibited unless explicitly specified by the instructor or TA. In the event that specific permission to use GenAI tools is given, it will (1) be explicitly stated, in writing, in the assignment, that such tools are allowed and (2) students must acknowledge any work that is not their own. The absence of either (1) explicit permission or (2) full acknowledgement by the student will be considered cheating and the student will earn zero points on the affected assignment(s). Use of these tools includes: citations for outside scholarship or information gathered from other sources, automatically paraphrasing existing work, generating text or media in response to assignment prompts, even if subsequently modified by the student, and any other use of these tools. “Full acknowledgment” means that students will, through highlighting or the use of track changes (or footnotes, etc.), clearly delineate any text or media that is generated by a GenAI tool. If you have any questions or concerns about this policy, contact your instructor before submitting any assignments. See UK-Adcnace instructional guidelines for more information.
